Five Geographical Groups

The survey did not treat the thirty districts as isolated units. Instead, it reorganized them into five geographical groups according to environmental conditions, especially landform, water access, and agricultural potential. This grouping allowed officials to compare different parts of Semipalatinsk Uezd and to evaluate whether particular districts were suitable for cultivation, settlement, or pastoral use.

The table below shows how the thirty districts were reorganized into five broader geographical groups.

This classification shows how the survey translated diverse pastoral landscapes into administrative and environmental categories. Rather than simply listing thirty districts, the survey grouped them into broader zones that could be compared and calculated. This step was important because the later calculation of land needs and “surplus land” depended on how officials understood the environmental capacity of each region.

However, this geographical grouping also simplified the complexity of Kazakh pastoral land use. Districts were categorized according to official environmental and agricultural criteria, but Kazakh mobility depended on seasonal movement, water availability, livestock needs, and local knowledge. For this reason, the five groups should be understood not only as geographic categories, but also as part of the survey’s administrative method for making pastoral land measurable.
